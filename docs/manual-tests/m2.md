# Manual test session after M2

A walk through everything built so far (M1: local DAW, M2: server and live sync) on a real screen
with real sound. The automated tests cover the logic; this session is for what they cannot see:
audio from the device, how the windows behave next to each other, and whether it feels right.

About 30 minutes. One machine is enough.

## 0. Preparation

- [ ] **0.1** Run `cargo test --workspace --exclude 'spike-*'`.
      Expect: every line says `ok`, none says `FAILED`.
- [ ] **0.2** Run `cargo build -p mdaw-server -p mdaw-client`.
      Expect: it finishes without errors.

## A. One client alone

Start it with `cargo run -p mdaw-client`.

- [ ] **A1** Look at the window.
      Expect: one track, "Track 1". At the top right, small grey text names your audio output and
      a sample rate. If it says "no audio device", sound will not work: note it and go on.
- [ ] **A2** In the lane right of "Track 1", drag from bar 1 to bar 3.
      Expect: a clip two bars long appears and is selected.
- [ ] **A3** Double-click the clip.
      Expect: the note editor at the bottom shows a piano keyboard and a grid for this clip.
- [ ] **A4** Press and hold the mouse on a key of the piano keyboard, then let go.
      Expect: **you hear the note** while you hold, and it ends when you let go.
- [ ] **A5** Click into the grid five or six times at different places and heights. Drag one of
      the notes somewhere else, and drag the right edge of another to make it longer.
      Expect: each click adds a note and sounds it. Notes move and stretch with the mouse and land
      on the grid.
- [ ] **A6** Press Space.
      Expect: the playhead runs, **you hear your notes in time**, and the level meter at the top
      right moves. Space again pauses.
- [ ] **A7** Click "Loop", press Space and let it go round twice. Then click "Stop" twice.
      Expect: playback repeats the loop region without a click or gap at the seam. After the
      second "Stop" the position reads `1.1.1`.
- [ ] **A8** Drag the tempo field (120.0 bpm) up to about 160 while it plays.
      Expect: it gets faster, without dropouts.
- [ ] **A9** Open the "Device" tab and turn "Cutoff" down and up while it plays.
      Expect: the sound gets duller and brighter. Double-clicking the knob puts it back.
- [ ] **A10** Press Ctrl+Z several times, then Ctrl+Shift+Z as often.
      Expect: your changes go away one gesture at a time, newest first, and come back in order.
- [ ] **A11** Click "+ Track", double-click the new track's name, type a name, press Enter. Click
      "M" on the first track while playing.
      Expect: the track is renamed. The muted track goes silent.
- [ ] **A12** Listen for a minute while playing and editing.
      Expect: no crackling, no dropouts, no stuck notes.

Close the window.

## B. Two clients on one server

Three terminals, in this order:

```sh
cargo xtask server --fresh     # terminal 1: leave it running
cargo xtask client Ann         # terminal 2
cargo xtask client Ben         # terminal 3
```

Put the two windows side by side so that you see both at once.

- [ ] **B1** Look at terminal 1 and at both windows.
      Expect: the server printed `listening on 127.0.0.1:7464` and a line starting `mdaw://`. The
      windows are titled "mdaw - Ann @ ..." and "mdaw - Ben @ ...". Both are empty (no tracks) and
      show in green "Online: Ann (you), Ben" or "Online: Ann, Ben (you)".
- [ ] **B2** In Ann's window click "+ Track". **Do not touch Ben's window.**
      Expect: "Track 1" appears in Ben's window within a moment, without the mouse being over it.
- [ ] **B3** Ann: draw a clip over bars 1 and 2 and double-click it. Ben: double-click the same
      clip. Ann: click three notes into the grid.
      Expect: each note shows up in Ben's note editor as it is made.
- [ ] **B4** Ben: drag one of Ann's notes to another pitch.
      Expect: it moves in Ann's window too.
- [ ] **B5** Ben: drag the clip in the arrangement one bar to the right. Ann: drag the track's
      volume field down to about -12 dB.
      Expect: the clip moves in Ann's window when Ben lets go. The volume in Ben's window follows
      Ann's while she drags.
- [ ] **B6** Ann: press Ctrl+Z once.
      Expect: Ann's volume change is undone in both windows. Ben's clip move stays: undo only
      takes back your own edits.
- [ ] **B7** Ann: press Space.
      Expect: Ann's window plays and sounds. Ben's does not start (see Known gaps). Press Space
      in Ben's window: it plays the same notes on its own.
- [ ] **B8** Ben: click "+ Track" and draw a clip on the new track. Ann: click the "×" on that
      track.
      Expect: the track disappears in both windows. Ann presses Ctrl+Z: the track and its clip
      are back in both.

## C. Holding a clip

- [ ] **C1** Ann: press the mouse on the clip, drag it a little and **keep the button down**. Look
      at Ben's window.
      Expect: the clip in Ben's window turns dark and carries a small yellow tag "Ann". It has
      not moved yet.
- [ ] **C2** Ann: keep holding for five seconds.
      Expect: the tag stays for as long as Ann holds.
- [ ] **C3** Ann: let go.
      Expect: the clip jumps to its new place in Ben's window, gets its colour back and loses
      the tag.

One mouse cannot hold in one window and drag in the other. That Ben's drag is refused while Ann
holds is checked by the automated test `a_clip_someone_else_drags_cannot_be_dragged`. With a
second person on a second machine: try it (step C4).

- [ ] **C4** *(optional, two machines)* While Ann holds the clip, Ben tries to drag it.
      Expect: the pointer shows "not allowed" over the clip and the drag does nothing.

## D. Losing the server

- [ ] **D1** Press Ctrl+C in terminal 1.
      Expect: within about ten seconds both windows show "Reconnecting" in yellow instead of
      "Online".
- [ ] **D2** Ann: add two notes. Ben: draw a new clip at bar 5.
      Expect: both edits show in the window they were made in. Ann's window reads "Reconnecting,
      1 edit waiting" or more; Ben's likewise. Neither window shows the other's edit.
- [ ] **D3** Terminal 1: `cargo xtask server` (**without** `--fresh`).
      Expect: within a few seconds both windows are "Online" again with both names, nothing is
      "waiting", and each window now has the other's edit too.
- [ ] **D4** Ann: press Ctrl+Z.
      Expect: the note Ann added last while offline goes away in both windows.

## E. Two edits to the same thing

Two people rarely change the same thing in the same millisecond on one machine, so this section
makes it happen with the server down.

- [ ] **E1** Press Ctrl+C in terminal 1 and wait for "Reconnecting" in both windows. Ann: drag the
      first clip to bar 3. Ben: drag the same clip to bar 7.
      Expect: each window shows its own position for the clip.
- [ ] **E2** Terminal 1: `cargo xtask server`. Wait for "Online".
      Expect: both windows show the clip at the same bar: either 3 or 7. One edit won.
- [ ] **E3** Open the "Changelog" tab in both windows.
      Expect: both list an overwritten edit (the newest is on top). In the loser's window it reads
      "you: start ... for a clip on "Track 1", overwritten by ..." with the other's name; in the
      winner's window the names are the other way round. The position named is the loser's.
- [ ] **E4** In either window click "Restore" on that line.
      Expect: the clip moves to the loser's position in both windows. The "Restore" button is
      now greyed out in both.
- [ ] **E5** In the window where you clicked "Restore", press Ctrl+Z.
      Expect: the clip goes back to the winner's position in both windows.

## F. A client goes away and comes back

- [ ] **F1** Close Ben's window.
      Expect: Ann's window shows "Online: Ann (you)" within a moment.
- [ ] **F2** Ann: add a track, a clip and a few notes. Then terminal 3: `cargo xtask client Ben`.
      Expect: Ben's window opens with everything, including what Ann did meanwhile. Both windows
      list both names. Ben's Changelog tab is empty (see Known gaps).
- [ ] **F3** Terminal 3: stop Ben hard with Ctrl+C in the terminal. Start it again right away.
      Expect: Ben is back with the whole project. Ann's list may show "Ben" twice for up to ten
      seconds, then once.
- [ ] **F4** Close both windows and stop the server with Ctrl+C. Start all three again as in
      section B, **without** `--fresh`.
      Expect: the project is as you left it.

## G. Joining with a wrong string

With the server running, in a free terminal (the join string is in `target/demo/join.txt`):

- [ ] **G1** `./target/debug/mdaw --join "$(cat target/demo/join.txt)x" --profile target/demo/profiles/zed`
      Expect: no window. The terminal says the server did not accept the session token.
- [ ] **G2** `./target/debug/mdaw --join 'mdaw://127.0.0.1:7464?fp=00&token=x'`
      Expect: no window. The terminal says the fingerprint must be 64 hex digits, and shows the
      usage text.
- [ ] **G3** Stop the server, then `cargo xtask client Zed`.
      Expect: no window. After about five seconds the terminal says it could not join because the
      server did not answer.

## Known gaps (not failures)

These are missing on purpose at this point. No need to report them.

- **Play and stop are per window.** A shared clock and transport come with M3.
- **Nothing is saved without a server.** `cargo run -p mdaw-client` alone opens a project that is
  gone when the window closes.
- **A held clip is only marked, its drag is not shown.** Others see where it went when it is
  dropped.
- **Only clips are held.** Notes, knobs and faders that someone else is dragging are not marked.
- **The changelog starts when you join.** A client that joins later does not see earlier entries,
  and it shows overwritten edits only: commits and listening samples come with M4.
- **Undo history is per window and is lost when the window closes.**
- **Notes that sound while their track is edited during playback are cut off.**
- **No checkout, no MIDI keyboard, no plugins.** M4, M5 and M6.
- **A refused edit** (yellow strip with "Dismiss" under the top bar) cannot be provoked from the
  UI yet. The automated tests cover it.
