# Manual test session after M5

M5 added live notes: what one player plays, from the computer keyboard or a MIDI device, is
heard by everyone else as it arrives, and recording turns it into a clip. A track someone has
checked out hears only its owner until the commit. The automated tests play notes between two
clients and a real server, through a stand-in for a slow and lossy network, check that no note
is left hanging, and check that a recording lands on the other client to the tick. This
session is for what they cannot judge: how it **sounds and feels**, a real MIDI device, and a
real bad network. Steps that a test covers are listed under
[Covered by automated tests](#covered-by-automated-tests).

About 20 minutes, one machine; section F needs `sudo`, section G a Windows machine.

## 0. Preparation

- [ ] **0.1** Run `cargo test --workspace --exclude 'spike-*'`.
      Expect: every line says `ok`, none says `FAILED`.
- [ ] **0.2** Run `cargo build -p mdaw-server -p mdaw-client`.
      Expect: it finishes without errors.

Three terminals:

```sh
cargo xtask server --fresh      # terminal 1: leave it running
cargo xtask client Ann          # terminal 2
cargo xtask client Ben          # terminal 3
```

Put the two windows side by side. Ann clicks "+ Track" once. Under the transport bar both
windows now show a second row: "Keys", "Octave C4", the MIDI input, and "Playing into Track 1".

## A. The computer keyboard

- [ ] **A1** Ann: click into an empty part of the arrangement, then hold A, then S, D, F, G, H,
      J, K one after the other.
      Expect: a C major scale from middle C. Each note starts **the moment** the key goes down
      and ends when it comes up. Holding a key does not repeat the note.
- [ ] **A2** Ann: play W, E, T, Y, U.
      Expect: the black keys between them.
- [ ] **A3** Ann: press X twice and play A, then Z three times and play A.
      Expect: the label says Octave C6, then C3; the note goes up two octaves, then down three.
- [ ] **A4** Ann: double-click the track name to rename it and type "bass".
      Expect: typing plays nothing. Enter keeps the name.
- [ ] **A5** Ann: hold A, and while holding it click into Ben's window.
      Expect: Ann's note ends when her window loses focus. Nothing keeps sounding.

## B. Playing together

- [ ] **B1** Ann: play a slow melody.
      Expect: Ben hears it shortly after Ann, as clean notes. Ann hears every note once, not
      twice. While Ann holds notes, Ben's track header says "Ann playing" next to the name.
- [ ] **B2** Ben: play a chord while Ann plays a melody, both on Track 1.
      Expect: each hears both parts. Neither one's notes cut the other's off, also on the same
      key.
- [ ] **B3** Ann: click "+ Track", select Track 2, and play.
      Expect: the label says "Playing into Track 2". Ben hears Ann on Track 2 (with Track 2's
      sound if it was changed in the Device tab), and the header of Track 2 says "Ann playing".
- [ ] **B4** Ann: hold a chord and close her window.
      Expect: on Ben's side Ann's notes end within about two seconds at most.

## C. A MIDI keyboard

## D. Recording

- [ ] **D1** Ann: click "Rec" (it turns red), press Space, play a short phrase in time with the
      beat, press Space again.
      Expect: while playing the strip says "Recording: n notes", counting up. On stop a clip
      called "Take" appears on Track 1 in both windows, covering whole bars, with the notes
      where they were played. Press Home and Space: the take plays back **in time**, sounding
      like what was played.
- [ ] **D2** Ann: switch Loop on (a one- or two-bar region), press Space, and play over a few
      passes before stopping.
      Expect: the notes of all passes land in the loop region, on top of each other.
- [ ] **D3** Ann: press Ctrl+Z.
      Expect: the whole take of D2 goes, in both windows, in one step.
- [ ] **D4** Ben: check out Track 1. Ann: record on Track 1 again.
      Expect: on stop, Ann's window says the track is checked out by Ben, and nothing is
      added.

## E. A checkout does not listen

- [ ] **E1** Ann: select Track 1 and play.
      Expect: Ben hears **nothing** of it on Track 1 (Ann hears herself). Ben's header shows no
      "Ann playing".
- [ ] **E2** Ben: play on Track 1.
      Expect: Ann hears Ben, and her header shows "Ben playing".
- [ ] **E3** Ben: click "Commit…", then "Commit". Ann: play on Track 1.

## F. A bad network

This slows down and drops packets on the loopback interface for every program on the machine
while it is on. Remember the last command.

```sh
sudo tc qdisc add dev lo root netem delay 40ms loss 5%    # 80 ms there and back
```

- [ ] **F1** Ann: play fast runs and chords for half a minute, also very short notes, then let
      go of everything.
      Expect: Ben hears Ann late, about a tenth of a second, with now and then a note missing or
      cut short. **No note keeps hanging**: at most a moment after Ann let go, Ben's side is
      silent, and the header no longer says "Ann playing".
- [ ] **F2** Ann: record a phrase while the transport plays (as in D1).
      Expect: the take appears on Ben's side a little later, with exactly the notes Ann
      played, at the same places, none missing.

```sh
sudo tc qdisc del dev lo root
```

## Covered by automated tests

|---|---|
| Notes reach the other client and end there; own notes heard once; same pitch from two players; a player who leaves is silenced | `live::notes_one_plays_sound_at_once_there_and_soon_on_the_other_client` |
| A checkout hears nobody else until the commit, and silences what sounded when it started | `live::a_checked_out_track_hears_nobody_else_until_the_commit` |
| The server does not pass others' notes to the checkout's owner | `live::the_server_does_not_pass_others_notes_to_the_owner_of_a_checkout` |
| 80 ms delay and 5 % loss: no note left hanging, silence long before the timeout | `live::over_a_slow_and_lossy_network_no_note_is_left_hanging` |
| A recording lands on the other client within a tick of where it was played; one undo step; nothing recorded while stopped or disarmed | `live::a_recording_lands_on_the_other_client_where_it_was_played` |
| Key layout, octave keys, held keys, shortcuts with Ctrl, focus loss, Keys switch | `ui::the_computer_keyboard_plays_the_selected_track` |

## Known gaps (not failures)

- **Notes go to the selected track**, or the first one if none is selected. One MIDI input at a
  time; its channel does not matter. Sustain pedal, pitch bend and other controllers are
  ignored.
- **The computer keyboard has one velocity** (100) and plays only while the arrangement window
  has focus and no text field is being edited.
- **Others are heard about one network delay late**, through the server; nothing makes up for
  the delay. Their notes play through this client's copy of the track: on a track checked out
  by someone else, that is the version from before the checkout.
- **The owner of a checkout is heard by the others** while playing into it, through their
  version of the track.
- **Recording needs the transport running.** A take covers one run of the transport and is
  written when it stops. Takes on a linked transport use the shared clock; unlinked or without
  a server, the private transport, a few milliseconds less precisely.
- **A note held when the connection drops** ends for the others within two seconds.
- Everything listed in the M4 protocol under Known gaps still holds, except the last point
  (piano keys heard only locally): notes played with the keyboard or a MIDI device are heard by
  everyone now. The piano keys of the note editor still only preview locally.
